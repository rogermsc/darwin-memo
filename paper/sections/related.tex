\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Memory poisoning, and where its defences sit.}
\citet{mpbench2026} systematize memory-poisoning attacks on LLM agents
and evaluate content-based detectors against them, reporting the split
that motivates this work: detectors average $63.6\%$ true-positive rate
on strong-signal payloads and $31.6\%$ on weak-signal ones---payloads
written to read like ordinary operational facts.
\citet{memsecbench2026} track poisoning through a lifecycle, from
persistence to consequence to repair, and identify selective repair as
the open stage: removing poison without destroying benign memory
alongside it. We adopt their Write--Execute--Forget checkpoint structure
as a protocol (\S\ref{sec:wef}), reimplemented rather than ported, and
we make no comparison to their numbers.

What both share with the wider defence literature is \emph{where} the
defence sits: at write, screening text before it is stored, or at
retrieval, screening entries before they are injected. Both read
content, and content is exactly what a weak-signal payload is
constructed to survive. Our threat model is orthogonal to that axis
rather than a better position on it. We do not ask whether the defence
can recognize the payload; we ask what the defence does when the
evidence it decides on has itself been tampered with. To our knowledge
no prior work models an adversary whose target is the curator rather
than the store, and the denial-of-memory consequence---a defence induced
to delete the memory it protects---appears unmeasured.

\paragraph{Memory as a model, and the frozen executive.}
\citet{quek2026memo} argue for keeping the executive LLM frozen and
placing new knowledge in a dedicated memory model, with a
reflection-style QA encoding pipeline and a multi-stage query protocol.
We adopt their separation of executive from memory and their encoding
structure (\S\ref{sec:method}), but our live store is a structured set of
self-contained QA entries rather than a fine-tuned model, because
selection must operate on \emph{individual} entries. We close the loop to
the parametric form as a secondary result: distilling the survivors into
a small model (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) shows that selection survives the
round-trip into weights.

\paragraph{Environment-mediated selection.}
\citet{dodgson2026survival} introduce sustainable self-training in which
behaviors persist by environmental viability under a conserved resource
and bounded memory, rather than by an explicit reward, precisely to avoid
the reward-hacking and semantic-drift that an optimizable proxy invites.
We take this principle as our foundation and apply it at the granularity
of memory entries: energy is the survival currency, upkeep is the cost of
existing, and the only income is a conserved, measured resource delta.
Where their agents propose executable behaviors, our entries propose
answers from which the environment reads an action, a smaller attack
surface with the same selection geometry.

\paragraph{Hand-designed memory scores.}
\citet{park2023generative} score memories for retrieval by a weighted sum
of recency, importance (an LLM-rated poignancy), and query relevance.
This is the canonical hand-designed memory-management heuristic, and we
include it as an \emph{external baseline} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}): we
repurpose the recency$+$importance core of their score as a retention
rule and ask whether a hand-designed salience signal curates as well as a
conserved-resource ledger. (Because a deterministic, always-in-CI arm
cannot call an LLM, we proxy their LLM-rated importance with consultation
frequency and omit their query-conditioned relevance term, which is a
retrieval property, not a retention one; we state this deviation
explicitly.) MemGPT \citep{packer2023memgpt} manages a context hierarchy
by paging rather than by selection; Reflexion \citep{shinn2023reflexion}
and ExpeL \citep{zhao2024expel} accumulate verbal lessons across episodes;
Voyager \citep{wang2023voyager} grows a skill library. None of these
removes entries by a conserved-resource criterion.

\paragraph{Bandits over retrieval policies.}
\citet{xu2026ael} present AEL, which uses Thompson-sampling bandit
learning to \emph{select among retrieval policies} and LLM-driven
reflection to evolve behavior across long episode sequences. AEL is a
positive method for using memory well, not an argument against energy
ledgers; we are careful not to attribute such a claim to it. What it
motivates is a sharp control for \emph{our} mechanism: if a simple
confidence bandit over per-entry outcomes curates as well as a conserved
ledger, the ledger is unnecessary. We therefore reconstruct a per-entry
successive-elimination bandit with a Hoeffding radius
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}) and report exactly the regime---one-sided
measurement noise---where it matches survival, and the three things it
cannot do (starve dead weight, kill promptly, survive lies that pay the
guilty).

\paragraph{LLM-curated memory and its failure mode.}
\citet{zhang2026faulty} document that memory banks an LLM continuously
rewrites and consolidates degrade over time: useful memories become
faulty as the rewriting accumulates, because the model grades and
restates plausibility rather than verified consequence. Their finding
motivates a control we call \texttt{judge\_settled}, which is \emph{one
operationalization} of LLM-controlled memory updates---keep/cull verdicts
from a local model in place of measured settlement. We are explicit that
this is our construction, not a reproduction of their setup, and we report
a mixed result (\S\ref{sec:experiments}): the degradation appears for one
local judge and not another at our scale, so the broad claim that
conserved-resource settlement is categorically better is one our
experiments do \emph{not} make. What is unambiguous is cost: judged
settlement is $10^4$--$10^6\times$ slower than reading a scalar the
environment already returns.

\paragraph{Continual-learning evaluation for coding agents.}
SWE-bench \citep{jimenez2024swebench} evaluates issue resolution on real
GitHub repositories; \citet{joshi2025swebenchcl} organize its
human-verified subset into per-repository continual-learning sequences.
We adopt SWE-Bench-CL as the real-task testbed for whether a lesson store
under conserved-resource selection produces a learning curve across a
sequence (\S\ref{sec:experiments}); the resource is the change in
passing-test count returned by the official evaluation harness.
