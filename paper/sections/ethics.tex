\section{Ethics and responsible disclosure}
\label{sec:ethics}

This paper describes an attack. Three things bound what we did with it.

\paragraph{What was attacked, and with whose knowledge.}
Two of the systems measured here are other people's:
\textsc{Mem0} (\S\ref{sec:mem0}) and \textsc{MemoryOS}
\citep{kang2025memoryos} (\S\ref{sec:memoryos}). Both were exercised against
their published source at a pinned commit, in local sandboxes, with synthetic
corpora we wrote. No hosted service was attacked, no account other than our
own was involved, and no third party's data was read, moved or destroyed at
any point.

Findings against \textsc{MemoryOS} were sent to the corresponding author
before this section was written, with the code paths, the scripts, and an
offer to correct anything we had described wrongly. The reply neither disputed
a finding nor requested an embargo, and invited fixes through the project's
repository. The correspondence, with the maintainer's address and our mail
identifiers removed, is archived in the artifact under
\texttt{docs/disclosure/}. We record that exchange as what it is: the
maintainers were given the findings in full and raised no objection. It is not
an endorsement, and every claim about their system rests on our own reading of
their published source.

Of the three \textsc{MemoryOS} findings, one is a defect with a patch to
submit; the other two describe a mechanism working exactly as designed, which
is why they are reported as properties of a design rather than as
vulnerabilities.

\paragraph{Why publishing this is the lower risk.}
The attack targets curators that remove entries \emph{automatically, by a rule,
from a signal}. Our survey of deployed systems (\S\ref{sec:mem0}) found that
none of them does this today: they decline to forget at all, forget only when
a caller asks, or forget only when a model agrees. The threat model is
therefore forward-looking with respect to the current ecosystem rather than a
vulnerability report against shipping software, and the mechanism most exposed
to it is the one this paper itself proposes. We would rather describe the
failure mode of a class of design before it is widely deployed than after.

That framing carries an obligation we have tried to meet: every defence we
measured is reported with the regime in which it fails, and the two negative
results (\S\ref{sec:limitations}) are stated as prominently as the positive
ones, because a reader deciding whether to deploy outcome-driven curation
needs the losses more than the wins.

\paragraph{What we are not releasing.}
The released artifact contains the attack harnesses used for the measurements
in this paper, against sandboxed local instances and synthetic corpora. It
contains no exploit for a hosted service, no credential, no scraped or
personal data, and no corpus derived from anyone's private notes. The
\textsc{Mem0} and \textsc{MemoryOS} scripts are released because a claim about
somebody else's curator is unverifiable without them, and because both
findings are already in the maintainers' hands.
