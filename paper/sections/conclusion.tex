\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether agent memory can curate itself without a model in the
loop, and answered: on tasks with a conserved, measurable resource, yes.
\textsc{darwin-memo} makes each memory entry pay energy upkeep and earn it
back only from measured outcomes along its provenance, so useless entries
starve, harmful ones are executed by the damage they cause, and
near-duplicates consolidate---with no reward model, no LLM judge, and no
human curation, at settlement cost four to six orders of magnitude below
an LLM curator. Rather than claim universal superiority, which our own
experiments refute, we contributed a \emph{regime map}: across two
environment families, ablations, one-sided and symmetric noise sweeps,
and literature controls (a retrieval bandit, an LLM judge, and a
Generative-Agents-style salience score), we located precisely where
conserved-resource selection dominates and where a one-line heuristic, a
confidence bandit, or a quarantine policy matches or beats it. The
properties that hold across the whole map---starving dead weight, killing
actionable poison within a cycle, tolerating one-sided lying
measurements, needing no labels, and costing almost nothing---are the
case for the mechanism, and the boundaries we publish are the case for
honesty about it. The open frontier is the real-task learning curve on
SWE-Bench-CL, whose harness we have validated end-to-end; that result
will decide whether the regime map transfers from instrumented
environments to the software-engineering tasks the mechanism was designed
for. All code, per-seed data, and pre-registered protocols are released
under a permissive license with manifest-checked reproducibility.
