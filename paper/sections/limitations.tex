\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state the threats to validity plainly, in the spirit of the
pre-registration above.

\paragraph{Outside the adversarial regime, the mechanism may be
unnecessary.}
We state the strongest argument against this paper because our own data
makes it. Three independent tests failed to find an advantage for the
energy ledger where no adversary is present. Pre-registered gates on
trajectory selection found it matched or beaten by a threshold counter
under i.i.d.\ noise, by a buffer frontier, and by a tuned EWMA under
drift. With a real model deciding adoption, a one-line eviction counter
revokes poison sooner (\S\ref{sec:wef}). On real software-engineering
tasks it shows no learning-curve advantage over a token-matched random
control, with the no-memory arm scoring highest of five
(\S\ref{sec:swebench}). Each has a local explanation---estimation
quality was never the claim, latency is the price of a buffer, the
benchmark's sequences are shorter than the mechanism's time
constant---and we give those explanations where they belong. But a
reader is entitled to the pattern rather than three separate
mitigations: absent an attacker, what we have demonstrated is leanness
and cost, not accuracy. The adversarial result is the one that survived
every attempt to find a cheaper substitute, and it is the claim we
would defend if forced to keep only one.

\paragraph{Vocabulary coupling.}
In the synthetic suites the corpus, the task prompts, and the
action-reading function were written in the same vocabulary by the same
hand. The crisp single-cycle poison kill lives in the near-exact
lexical-match regime that coupling creates. We measure outside it with
paraphrase probes scored by provenance rather than by the keyword
reader: harmful paraphrases stay safe (silence is conservative), but the
lexical arm grounds only a third of benign paraphrases, and an embedding
retriever doubles that. The headline numbers should be read as
within-distribution, with the paraphrase columns as the
out-of-distribution honesty check. An LLM-encoded corpus with
independently authored probes would close this gap and is the most
important synthetic-side improvement.

\paragraph{Synthetic environments, and a real-task leg that came back null.}
The headline, noise, ablation, and control results are on two synthetic
environment families. They are deliberately adversarial in opposite
directions (storage prices inaction; the test-suite family does not), and
we flag every family-dependent conclusion rather than averaging it, but
they remain synthetic. The SWE-Bench-CL leg (\S\ref{sec:swebench}) exists
to move the central claim onto real software-engineering tasks, and it
did not support it: no learning-curve advantage over a token-matched
random control, $p = 0.50$ over six paired worlds, with the no-memory arm
scoring highest of the five.

We first attributed that to the benchmark rather than the mechanism: the
pilot's sequences are $19$ and $22$ tasks against a $20$-cycle
starvation horizon, and only $10$ of the $246$ lessons minted by
upkeep-charging arms ever died, so the removal the ledger depends on
could not occur. The attribution was arithmetic rather than
interpretation, but it was still made after seeing the result, which is
the weakest kind---so we tested it. A second matrix over \texttt{django}
and \texttt{sympy}, $50$ tasks each at $2.5\times$ the horizon, moved
starvation from $4\%$ of minted lessons to $52\%$ ($155$ of $300$) and
held the population at $24$ where \texttt{keep\_everything} reaches
$50$.

\emph{The excuse did not survive its own test.} With removal fully
active the comparison is $+0.027$ $[-0.020, +0.073]$, $p = 0.50$; on
\texttt{django} alone, where per-seed variance is smallest, it is
$-0.013$ with $p = 1.0000$. Halving the store changed capability by
nothing measurable, and \texttt{memory\_off} again scored highest. Across
four sequences and $2{,}115$ evaluated tasks, no memory arm has beaten
no memory at all. We therefore retract the benchmark explanation as a
defence of the mechanism and keep it only as a description of the pilot.
The honest status is that conserved-resource selection improves
\emph{leanness} on real tasks---demonstrably, half the store for equal
capability---and that its effect on task outcomes is unvalidated and, on
the evidence we have, plausibly zero. Two further limits below
(retrieval recall, replicate count) would each depress a real effect, so
this null should
not be read as an upper bound on the mechanism either.

\paragraph{The task stream is not known to contain reusable work.}
A stronger version of that caveat has since been made general.
\citet{agentcl2026} argue that lifelong-adaptation benchmarks rest on
``naive task streams'' in which nothing guarantees that an earlier
sub-solution, piece of evidence, or workflow is reusable later, and
propose \emph{controlled} streams in which reuse is constructed by
design. SWE-Bench-CL orders tasks within a repository by date and by
estimated fix time; it does not construct reuse. If lesson $n$ is not
usable at task $n+k$, no curation policy can separate itself from
\texttt{memory\_off}, and our null is then a measurement of the stream
rather than of the ledger. We cannot distinguish these two readings with
the data we have, and we no longer claim to: a benchmark without
constructed reuse cannot tell ``selection did not help'' apart from
``there was nothing to reuse.''
\citet{cl2memory2026} locate a second bound in the same place, finding
that memory relocates the stability--plasticity dilemma to retrieval
rather than dissolving it, and that abstract procedural memories transfer
where detailed trajectories do not. Our entries are detailed QA pairs.

\paragraph{No representation arm.}
Every arm in this paper stores the same thing---a question, an answer,
and provenance---and varies only \emph{which} entries survive. The
comparison that is now unavoidable is against strategy-level memory:
\citet{reasoningbank2026} report $54.0 \rightarrow 57.4$ resolve on
SWE-Bench-Verified with Gemini-2.5-pro and $34.2 \rightarrow 38.8$ with
Gemini-2.5-flash, storing distilled reasoning strategies rather than
episodes. Their selection is an LLM judging its own trajectories, which
this design refuses; but representation and selection are separate axes
and we have varied only one of them. Whether our null reflects the
selection rule or the thing being selected is untested, and it is
testable without adopting a judge.

\paragraph{Scale of the parametric result.}
The distillation arm is a 0.5B model on a small corpus with deliberately
distinctive poison tokens, so reproduction is unambiguous. It is a clean
existence proof that selection survives the round-trip into weights, not
a scaling law, and a corpus whose poison is reachable by generalizing
benign patterns would not separate as cleanly. We present it as a
secondary result and do not generalize it.

\paragraph{The action reader is part of the measurement.}
The environment decides whether an answer requested an action by reading
its text, and that reader is narrower than the language models actually
produce: it has ``safe to delete'' and a model writes ``deleting
store-1.db is safe'', which parses as silence. An action the model
plainly decided then never executes, is never measured, and yields no
selection signal. We found this from inside while building the
model-in-the-loop suite, and we handle it by reading LLM-mode answers
with paraphrases of the same decision rather than by widening the shared
vocabulary, which would silently move every committed deterministic
number. The disagreement rate between the two readers is reported with
the suite and bounds every LLM-mode claim we make. It is a real threat
to validity for any benchmark of this shape: a mechanism can look
conservative when its environment is merely deaf.

\paragraph{Three replicates, and what the seed does not control.}
In the deterministic suites a seed fixes the entire world, so a paired
test compares two policies inside one realization. That is not true of
the real-task leg. The pilot's seed feeds exactly one generator---the
lesson choice in \texttt{random\_matched}---and the endpoint is called at
temperature $0$ with no seed parameter, so for every other arm the three
``seeds'' are three draws of endpoint nondeterminism rather than
controlled replicates. \texttt{memory\_off}, which holds no store at all
and therefore varies across seeds by sampling alone, resolved $7$, $10$,
and $9$ of the 19 pytest tasks: about $8$ points of resolve-rate standard
deviation on an identical configuration. Pairing on (sequence, seed)
still controls the task sequence and its ordering, which is worth having,
but it does not control the sampling. At three replicates and that
variance, only a large memory effect clears the noise, and any null we
report on real tasks should be read as underpowered rather than as
evidence of absence.

The adversarial leg supplies a second, much tighter estimate of the same
quantity, and we flag the disagreement rather than averaging it. On
\texttt{django}, \texttt{keep\_everything} never settles, so its store
and therefore its prompts are identical with and without the attack; it
resolved identically in all three seeds ($23/23$, $23/23$, $22/22$),
bounding endpoint nondeterminism there at about one task in fifty
(\S\ref{sec:swebench-attack}). That is not the $8$ points above, and the
two are not the same measurement: the pilot figure is a cross-seed
spread over a $19$-task sequence in a different repository, while this
one is a within-world comparison of prompt-identical runs over $50$
tasks. The honest reading is that \emph{paired} comparisons inside a
world are far tighter than the pilot's spread suggests, and that the
pilot's spread still bounds what a cross-seed comparison can resolve.
Neither licenses reading the other. Passing the endpoint's seed parameter, where the
provider offers one, and raising the replicate count are both cheap and
are the first things to do before this leg is treated as settled.

\paragraph{The real-task leg is bounded by its retriever.}
On SWE-Bench-CL the model edits whatever BM25 put in front of it, and at
the configuration we report that is the correct file on $74\%$ of the
pytest sequence (\S\ref{sec:swebench}). The remaining quarter is
unanswerable for every arm, so the resolve rates are depressed by an
amount that has nothing to do with memory. Because the retrieval query
is issue-only and identical across arms, this costs statistical power
rather than biasing the comparison---but it does mean our real-task
numbers are a lower bound on what the same memory mechanism would show
with a stronger code retriever, and that a null result there would be
consistent with either an absent memory effect or a retrieval ceiling.
We have since measured the ceiling directly (no model and no evaluation
harness required, since the question is only whether the prompt contains
the file): over the full pilot, BM25 puts at least one gold file in
context for $0.78$ of tasks at the budget the pilot ran, and $0.41$ at
the original budget, while the oracle control reaches every gold file in
every task. In counts, \textbf{9 of the 41 pilot tasks never showed the
model the file it had to patch}, and $24$ of $41$ did not at the original
budget. That bounds the null rather than rescuing it: a fifth of the
pilot could not have shown a memory effect under any curation policy, and
the remaining $32$ tasks still produced no separation. What we have not
run is the oracle arm end to end --- the recall measurement says the
files can be reached, not what the arms score once they are.

A second bound, independent of the first and visible without any new
run, sits in the shape of the pre-registered metric. Our headline cell
was the second-half-minus-first-half improvement, and per sequence
position every arm resolves every task in positions $1$--$4$ and
\emph{nothing at all} from position $17$ onward --- including
\texttt{memory\_off}, which carries no lessons. The curriculum ramps
monotonically into a region no arm solves, so the second half has a
floor of zero that no curation policy can improve on, and all five arms
post a strongly negative curve ($-0.535$ to $-0.606$). The metric was
designed on the assumption that the second half was winnable. It was
not, which is a defect in our experimental design rather than a finding
about memory, and it means the leg could not have detected an effect of
the size we were looking for even had one been present.

\paragraph{The filter control is a reconstruction.}
The write-time detector in \S\ref{sec:memsec} is a keyword and
authority-marker rule we wrote, not a port of any published system. We
give it the shape the published filters have and report its TPR/FPR in
every row so its behaviour is visible rather than assumed, but a trained
classifier would land elsewhere on the weak-signal class: the best
off-the-shelf detector in \citet{mpbench2026} reports $42.50\%$ there,
and the weakest $10.74\%$, against the $0\%$ our reconstruction achieves. The
claim the experiment supports is directional---content inspection
degrades sharply on payloads written to look ordinary, where consequence
does not---and not a numerical comparison against any named filter.

\paragraph{Consolidation is a laundering surface.}
Our own merge machinery carried a poisoned fragment to the end of every
seed in one cell by pooling it with a correct entry that earns
(\S\ref{sec:memsec}). The surviving text advises no action, so nothing
punishes it, but it remains retrievable inside a well-fed entry. Any
mechanism that consolidates memories inherits this channel, and we have
not evaluated the obvious fix (refusing to merge across trust
boundaries).

\paragraph{Silence as a harbor.}
In environments where inaction produces no measurement, a conservative
entry can dodge lying measurements simply by staying silent, which
flatters safety metrics. Environments that price inaction would expose
entries to lies the current design avoids, and would also likely reverse
the second-family loss (\S\ref{sec:regime}, Axis 3).

\paragraph{Local-judge scale.}
The LLM-judge control uses small (3--4B) local models at five seeds; a
frontier judge might hold capability where one local judge dropped it.
We therefore make no categorical claim that conserved-resource settlement
beats judge settlement on quality---only that it is orders of magnitude
cheaper, which is robust.
