\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state the threats to validity plainly, in the spirit of the
pre-registration above.

\paragraph{Outside the adversarial regime, the mechanism may be
unnecessary.}
We state the strongest argument against this paper because our own data
makes it. Three independent tests failed to find an advantage for the
energy ledger where no adversary is present. Pre-registered gates on
trajectory selection found it matched or beaten by a threshold counter
under i.i.d.\ noise, by a buffer frontier, and by a tuned EWMA under
drift. With a real model deciding adoption, a one-line eviction counter
revokes poison sooner (\S\ref{sec:wef}). On real software-engineering
tasks it shows no learning-curve advantage over a token-matched random
control, with the no-memory arm scoring highest of five
(\S\ref{sec:swebench}). Each has a local explanation---estimation
quality was never the claim, latency is the price of a buffer, the
benchmark's sequences are shorter than the mechanism's time
constant---and we give those explanations where they belong. But a
reader is entitled to the pattern rather than three separate
mitigations: absent an attacker, what we have demonstrated is leanness
and cost, not accuracy. The adversarial result is the one that survived
every attempt to find a cheaper substitute, and it is the claim we
would defend if forced to keep only one.

\paragraph{The real-task adversarial result is scoped to one repository.}
The pre-registered design was six paired worlds over two SWE-Bench-CL
sequences, and we ran all six. The burial magnitude replicates on
\texttt{django} at three seeds and is absent on \texttt{sympy} at three,
for a reason we can state but did not predict: the counter already buries
four times as much benign memory there with no adversary present
(\S\ref{sec:swebench-attack}). The ordering that held in five worlds
reverses in the sixth, leaving $5/6$ and $p = 0.0625$ where six worlds
were run to reach $0.031$. Two sequences chosen for having enough tasks
cannot tell us which of them is typical, so the honest scope of the
real-task adversarial leg is one repository, and the mechanism argument
of \S\ref{sec:adversary} rests on the synthetic suite plus a
\texttt{django}-shaped confirmation rather than on a replicated law.

\paragraph{Vocabulary coupling.}
In the synthetic suites the corpus, the task prompts, and the
action-reading function were written in the same vocabulary by the same
hand. The crisp single-cycle poison kill lives in the near-exact
lexical-match regime that coupling creates. We measure outside it with
paraphrase probes scored by provenance rather than by the keyword
reader: harmful paraphrases stay safe (silence is conservative), but the
lexical arm grounds only a third of benign paraphrases, and an embedding
retriever doubles that. The headline numbers should be read as
within-distribution, with the paraphrase columns as the
out-of-distribution honesty check. An LLM-encoded corpus with
independently authored probes would close this gap and is the most
important synthetic-side improvement.

\paragraph{Synthetic environments, and a real-task leg that came back null.}
The headline, noise, ablation, and control results are on two synthetic
environment families. They are deliberately adversarial in opposite
directions (storage prices inaction; the test-suite family does not), and
we flag every family-dependent conclusion rather than averaging it, but
they remain synthetic. The SWE-Bench-CL leg (\S\ref{sec:swebench}) exists
to move the central claim onto real software-engineering tasks, and it
did not support it: no learning-curve advantage over a token-matched
random control, $p = 0.50$ over six paired worlds, with the no-memory arm
scoring highest of the five.

We first attributed that to the benchmark rather than the mechanism: the
pilot's sequences are $19$ and $22$ tasks against a $20$-cycle
starvation horizon, and only $10$ of the $246$ lessons minted by
upkeep-charging arms ever died, so the removal the ledger depends on
could not occur. The attribution was arithmetic rather than
interpretation, but it was still made after seeing the result, which is
the weakest kind---so we tested it. A second matrix over \texttt{django}
and \texttt{sympy}, $50$ tasks each at $2.5\times$ the horizon, moved
starvation from $4\%$ of minted lessons to $52\%$ ($155$ of $300$) and
held the population at $24$ where \texttt{keep\_everything} reaches
$50$.

\emph{The excuse did not survive its own test.} With removal fully
active the comparison is $+0.027$ $[-0.020, +0.073]$, $p = 0.50$; on
\texttt{django} alone, where per-seed variance is smallest, it is
$-0.013$ with $p = 1.0000$. Halving the store changed capability by
nothing measurable, and \texttt{memory\_off} again scored highest. Across
four sequences and $2{,}115$ evaluated tasks, no memory arm has beaten
no memory at all. We therefore retract the benchmark explanation as a
defence of the mechanism and keep it only as a description of the pilot.
The honest status is that conserved-resource selection improves
\emph{leanness} on real tasks---demonstrably, half the store for equal
capability---and that its effect on task outcomes is unvalidated and, on
the evidence we have, plausibly zero. Two further limits below
(retrieval recall, replicate count) would each depress a real effect, so
this null should
not be read as an upper bound on the mechanism either.

\paragraph{The task stream is not known to contain reusable work.}
A stronger version of that caveat has since been made general.
\citet{agentcl2026} argue that lifelong-adaptation benchmarks rest on
``naive task streams'' in which nothing guarantees that an earlier
sub-solution, piece of evidence, or workflow is reusable later, and
propose \emph{controlled} streams in which reuse is constructed by
design. SWE-Bench-CL orders tasks within a repository by date and by
estimated fix time; it does not construct reuse. If lesson $n$ is not
usable at task $n+k$, no curation policy can separate itself from
\texttt{memory\_off}, and our null is then a measurement of the stream
rather than of the ledger. We cannot distinguish these two readings with
the data we have, and we no longer claim to: a benchmark without
constructed reuse cannot tell ``selection did not help'' apart from
``there was nothing to reuse.''
\citet{cl2memory2026} locate a second bound in the same place, finding
that memory relocates the stability--plasticity dilemma to retrieval
rather than dissolving it, and that abstract procedural memories transfer
where detailed trajectories do not. Our entries are detailed QA pairs.

\paragraph{No representation arm.}
Every arm in this paper stores the same thing---a question, an answer,
and provenance---and varies only \emph{which} entries survive. The
comparison that is now unavoidable is against strategy-level memory:
\citet{reasoningbank2026} report $54.0 \rightarrow 57.4$ resolve on
SWE-Bench-Verified with Gemini-2.5-pro and $34.2 \rightarrow 38.8$ with
Gemini-2.5-flash, storing distilled reasoning strategies rather than
episodes. Their selection is an LLM judging its own trajectories, which
this design refuses; but representation and selection are separate axes
and we have varied only one of them. Whether our null reflects the
selection rule or the thing being selected is untested, and it is
testable without adopting a judge.

\paragraph{Scale of the parametric result.}
The distillation arm is a 0.5B model on a small corpus with deliberately
distinctive poison tokens, so reproduction is unambiguous. It is a clean
existence proof that selection survives the round-trip into weights, not
a scaling law, and a corpus whose poison is reachable by generalizing
benign patterns would not separate as cleanly. We present it as a
secondary result and do not generalize it.

\paragraph{The action reader is part of the measurement.}
The environment decides whether an answer requested an action by reading
its text, and that reader is narrower than the language models actually
produce: it has ``safe to delete'' and a model writes ``deleting
store-1.db is safe'', which parses as silence. An action the model
plainly decided then never executes, is never measured, and yields no
selection signal. We found this from inside while building the
model-in-the-loop suite, and we handle it by reading LLM-mode answers
with paraphrases of the same decision rather than by widening the shared
vocabulary, which would silently move every committed deterministic
number. The disagreement rate between the two readers is reported with
the suite and bounds every LLM-mode claim we make. It is a real threat
to validity for any benchmark of this shape: a mechanism can look
conservative when its environment is merely deaf.

\paragraph{Three replicates, and what the seed does not control.}
In the deterministic suites a seed fixes the entire world, so a paired
test compares two policies inside one realization. That is not true of
the real-task leg. The pilot's seed feeds exactly one generator---the
lesson choice in \texttt{random\_matched}---and the endpoint is called at
temperature $0$ with no seed parameter, so for every other arm the three
``seeds'' are three draws of endpoint nondeterminism rather than
controlled replicates. \texttt{memory\_off}, which holds no store at all
and therefore varies across seeds by sampling alone, resolved $7$, $10$,
and $9$ of the 19 pytest tasks: about $8$ points of resolve-rate standard
deviation on an identical configuration. Pairing on (sequence, seed)
still controls the task sequence and its ordering, which is worth having,
but it does not control the sampling. At three replicates and that
variance, only a large memory effect clears the noise, and any null we
report on real tasks should be read as underpowered rather than as
evidence of absence.

The adversarial leg supplies a second, much tighter estimate of the same
quantity, and we flag the disagreement rather than averaging it. On
\texttt{django}, \texttt{keep\_everything} never settles, so its store
and therefore its prompts are identical with and without the attack; it
resolved identically in all three seeds ($23/23$, $23/23$, $22/22$),
bounding endpoint nondeterminism there at about one task in fifty
(\S\ref{sec:swebench-attack}). That is not the $8$ points above, and the
two are not the same measurement: the pilot figure is a cross-seed
spread over a $19$-task sequence in a different repository, while this
one is a within-world comparison of prompt-identical runs over $50$
tasks. The honest reading is that \emph{paired} comparisons inside a
world are far tighter than the pilot's spread suggests, and that the
pilot's spread still bounds what a cross-seed comparison can resolve.
Neither licenses reading the other. Passing the endpoint's seed parameter, where the
provider offers one, and raising the replicate count are both cheap and
are the first things to do before this leg is treated as settled.

\paragraph{The real-task leg is bounded by its retriever.}
On SWE-Bench-CL the model edits whatever BM25 put in front of it, and at
the configuration we report that is the correct file on $74\%$ of the
pytest sequence (\S\ref{sec:swebench}). The remaining quarter is
unanswerable for every arm, so the resolve rates are depressed by an
amount that has nothing to do with memory. Because the retrieval query
is issue-only and identical across arms, this costs statistical power
rather than biasing the comparison---but it does mean our real-task
numbers are a lower bound on what the same memory mechanism would show
with a stronger code retriever, and that a null result there would be
consistent with either an absent memory effect or a retrieval ceiling.
We have since measured the ceiling directly (no model and no evaluation
harness required, since the question is only whether the prompt contains
the file): over the full pilot, BM25 puts at least one gold file in
context for $0.78$ of tasks at the budget the pilot ran, and $0.41$ at
the original budget, while the oracle control reaches every gold file in
every task. In counts, \textbf{9 of the 41 pilot tasks never showed the
model the file it had to patch}, and $24$ of $41$ did not at the original
budget. That bounds the null rather than rescuing it: a fifth of the
pilot could not have shown a memory effect under any curation policy, and
the remaining $32$ tasks still produced no separation. What we have not
run is the oracle arm end to end --- the recall measurement says the
files can be reached, not what the arms score once they are.

A second bound applies to the \emph{pilot} sequences specifically, and
it is worth separating from the leg as a whole because the two carry
very different weight. On the two short pilot sequences (\texttt{pytest}
at $19$ tasks, \texttt{astropy} at $22$), per sequence position every
arm resolves every task in positions $1$--$4$ and \emph{nothing at all}
from position $17$ onward --- including \texttt{memory\_off}, which
carries no lessons. That curriculum ramps monotonically into a region no
arm solves, so its second half has a floor of zero that no curation
policy can improve on, and all five arms post a strongly negative curve
($-0.535$ to $-0.606$). On those two sequences the pre-registered
second-half metric could not have detected an effect had one been
present, and we do not read them as evidence about memory.

The main matrix does not share that defect, which is why the null rests
on it. The $50$-task \texttt{django} and \texttt{sympy} sequences run
$300$ evaluated tasks per arm, their per-position resolve rates
fluctuate across the full length rather than collapsing (positions in
the forties still resolve at $0.5$--$1.0$), and the first-to-second-half
change is small for every arm ($-0.03$ to $-0.11$) rather than
catastrophic. There the comparison is properly powered and properly
floored: \texttt{memory\_off} $0.360$, \texttt{memory\_on} $0.350$,
\texttt{random\_matched} $0.350$, curve difference $+0.027$
$[-0.020, +0.073]$, $p = 0.50$. The null is a measurement on that
matrix, and a non-measurement on the pilot; conflating the two would
either overstate the evidence or discard it, and both would be wrong.

\paragraph{The filter control is a reconstruction.}
The write-time detector in \S\ref{sec:memsec} is a keyword and
authority-marker rule we wrote, not a port of any published system. We
give it the shape the published filters have and report its TPR/FPR in
every row so its behaviour is visible rather than assumed, but a trained
classifier would land elsewhere on the weak-signal class: the best
off-the-shelf detector in \citet{mpbench2026} reports $42.50\%$ there,
and the weakest $10.74\%$, against the $0\%$ our reconstruction achieves. The
claim the experiment supports is directional---content inspection
degrades sharply on payloads written to look ordinary, where consequence
does not---and not a numerical comparison against any named filter.

\paragraph{Our own defence metric flattered three readings before we
audited it.}
The natural way to score a memory defence is to ask whether the bad thing
is still happening---here, whether any surviving poisoned entry currently
advises action. That question is silent about a mechanism that never
removed the payload and merely never acted on it, and it read as immunity
three separate times in this work: once on real tasks, where consolidation
made fifty poisoned sources report as one live entry
(\S\ref{sec:swebench-attack}); once on the persistence adversary, where
flat counter kill rates were published as resistance before the
provenance count showed the counters had never eliminated anything
(\S\ref{sec:persistence}); and once in the headline table, where the
``tie'' with a one-line counter held on outcomes and on kill rate while
the counter carried two poisoned entries to the end of every seed. The
first two corrections cost us a conclusion; the third turned out to
favour us, which is exactly why the pattern is worth stating as a threat
to validity rather than as three fixed bugs. We now report both readings
in every table and require the provenance count in the committed
evidence, but the general lesson is not specific to poison: any metric of
the form ``is the bad thing currently happening'' will flatter whichever
mechanism never removed the bad thing, and needs a count of what is still
\emph{present} beside it. Related: the code pointer in our results
manifest went unvalidated long enough for four of twenty-one entries to
name commits absent from the repository and one to name a commit
predating the suite that produced its file. The run grid was bound by a
hash the whole time; the code that walked it was not. Those entries are
repaired and the pointer is now bound in CI---every entry must name an
ancestor of published history, in a tree whose \texttt{bench/run.py}
already offered the suite that produced its file---but the guard that
sat over them while they were broken \emph{passed} them, because it
asked whether the sha resolved in the \emph{local} object store, which a
squash-discarded branch commit does forever. That is the same failure as
the metric above in a different costume: a check that can succeed for a
reason unavailable to the reader is reporting on something other than
what it names.

\paragraph{The withholding results now span two families, and every
other result here is still one horizon deep.} The first version of this
paragraph said the measurement that would settle whether the ledger's
amnesia is corpus-specific had not been built. It has since been built
and run (Table~\ref{tab:withholding-testsuite}), and it settled the
direction rather than the size: survival still has the best cumulative
delta at total suppression, but by $8\%$ rather than by threefold, and
on the family where a counter beats it outright when unattacked
(\S\ref{sec:second-env}). Two families is not many, and both scored
inaction at exactly zero --- the property the result actually rested on.
That is now measured too, and it is where the conclusion breaks: priced
inaction reverses it (\S\ref{sec:rent}, Table~\ref{tab:rent}), at a rent
of about $0.4$ of what the declined action is worth. We keep the
withholding claim in the scoped form the sweep leaves it in: at total
suppression the ledger is the only arm that removes the poison, and the
only one whose kill rate is not $0.00$, but it no longer has the best
cumulative outcome once standing still is billed. ``Amnesia is cheaper''
was never a property of amnesia. It was a property of an environment
that did not charge for it, and we said so as though it were the former.

We have since run the lying adversary across the same axis, and it
narrows the claim above rather than widening it. The reversal is not
``amnesia is expensive''; it is that an empty store has no answers, and
rent bills exactly that --- the rent an arm pays is its decline count,
measured, to two decimal places of its cum-delta slope. Against an
attacker the ledger can still survive, the same pricing pays it
\emph{more}: at a budget-2 liar it is rank 1 at every rent on both
horizons, and its margin over the counter widens from $+24.76$M to
$+50.42$M as rent rises, in $30/30$ seeds at every step. What rent
punishes is the state of having nothing useful left, which total
suppression produces and a working store does not.

The cost that survives all of this is not about attackers at all, and it
is the one we would flag to a practitioner. \emph{Leanness is billed.}
Our real-task claim is that conserved-resource selection buys leanness
--- demonstrably, half the store for equal capability. Where inaction is
priced, half the store is a win only if the half you kept answers the
questions: \texttt{survival} declines twice as often as
\texttt{keep\_everything} here and pays $2.8\times$ the rent for it. It
wins anyway, on the poison, but that is a trade rather than a free
lunch, and an environment with a cheaper poison and a higher rent would
settle it the other way.

The horizon has now changed a conclusion twice, and the second time it
changed whether there was one. Under withholding, benign retention reads
$0.92$ at 30 cycles and $0.44$ at 60. On the rented test-suite family
every arm is flat at 30 cycles and the ledger's bill only begins around
cycle 49, so a 30-cycle grid there does not report a smaller effect --- it
reports no effect, which is a false negative (\S\ref{sec:rent}). Every
other deterministic result here runs 30 cycles, four past the
$\text{spawn}/\text{upkeep}$ starvation cliff at 20, and we now have one
measured case of an effect that is entirely invisible at that horizon.
Which of those results would move at 60 is unknown and is no longer a
theoretical worry.

\paragraph{The withholding budget is not spent equally across arms.} The
adversary spends only on measurable outcomes, and a shrinking store
produces fewer of them: at total suppression it fires 193 suppressions
against survival and 289 against every other arm. The comparison is
therefore at matched \emph{capacity}, not matched \emph{fired}, and
matched-fired is unreachable here because the attack is self-limiting
precisely when it is winning. This is the same asymmetry
\S\ref{sec:adversary} reports for the lying adversary, in a sharper form.
It is also a property of unpriced inaction rather than of the attack: in
the rented environment every task returns a measured outcome, all five
arms saturate their budget at every non-zero rent, and the asymmetry
does not shrink but disappears (\S\ref{sec:rent}).

\paragraph{Consolidation is a laundering surface, and the boundary is
crossed upstream of it.}
A poisoned fragment reaches the end of every seed in one cell carrying
benign provenance (\S\ref{sec:memsec}). We have now evaluated the fix we
named here, and both halves of our description of the problem were wrong
(\S\ref{sec:merge-policy}). The entry never earns --- its \texttt{uses}
is zero and its energy is pooled from its own poisoned siblings --- and
it is not permanent, starving at cycle 59. More importantly the
\emph{encoder} creates the mixed-provenance entry, not the merge: the
no-defence arm, which consolidates nothing, carries two of them, because
the payload claims authority from an entity the corpus already trusts
and lands in that entity's cross-document entry. Consolidation's part is
to pool the fragment's energy and so extend its runway from 20 cycles to
59.

Refusing to merge across trust boundaries therefore removes the runway
rather than the entry. The strict form does close the channel outright
($10/10$ seeds) for one further unanswered probe, and the natural form
--- one shared source --- refuses nothing at all, because every merge
here already shares one. What is still unevaluated is the fix that would
address the actual crossing: an encoder that will not pool text from
different documents into one entity or summary entry. That is a change
to how memories are written rather than to how they are merged, and it
would move every committed number in this paper.

\paragraph{Silence as a harbor.}
In environments where inaction produces no measurement, a conservative
entry can dodge lying measurements simply by staying silent, which
flatters safety metrics. Both halves are now measured on the storage
corpus, and the prediction was right about the exposure and wrong about
who it costs (\S\ref{sec:rent}). Against a withholder the harbor is real
and pricing inaction closes it outright. Against a liar the exposure is
sharper than we predicted --- a decline is negative, so an inverted
measurement \emph{pays} it, and the attacker's whole extra budget goes
into paying conservatism rather than into new blame --- but the arm it
ruins is the counter, not the ledger, because the counter is the one
left with nothing useful to say. We did not anticipate that direction
and would not have found it without running the interior budget: at
total lying every arm's kill rate is $0.00$ and the ordering is
uninformative.

The third claim in that paragraph was that pricing inaction would also
likely reverse the second-family loss (\S\ref{sec:regime}, Axis 3). We
have now measured it and it is wrong: rent widens that loss at 60 cycles
and does nothing to it at 30 (\S\ref{sec:rent}). The counters cannot be
billed on that family at all, because its corpus is deliberately
redundant and a spare twin is always available to answer with; the
ledger consolidates the twins away and is the only arm that ever has
nothing to say. We predicted rent would reward the ledger's restraint.
It charges for it.

The shape of the price is now covered too, and it removes the obvious
way the two results above could have been artifacts. A flat rate is not
an economy anyone runs: real quotas exempt what you are required to
retain. Billing by category at a matched total (\S\ref{sec:rent},
Table~\ref{tab:rent-tiers}) shows the flat rate was charging the
unattacked column entirely for \emph{correct} answers --- every negative
outcome it produced there is a refusal to delete a protected file, and
no arm ever declines a disposable one --- so under a policy-shaped
quota the unattacked column is bit-identical to the unpriced world for
every arm, and the only thing rent bills is a store the attacker has
emptied. That sharpens the rule rather than softening it, and it makes
the total-suppression reversal worse rather than better: the crossing
rent moves from $0.427$ to $0.357$ and the ledger's loss against the
do-nothing floor roughly doubles.

Two things this leaves open. The tiers vary the price by category, which
is the dimension this corpus has; age and tier, the other two a real
quota uses, would need entries with a lifetime the environment can see.
And pricing a category at zero also makes it unattackable, since the
withholder spends only where $\text{true} \neq 0$, so shape and attack
surface are confounded in these grids by construction. The confound runs
against the result rather than producing it, but separating them would
need an adversary whose budget is spent independently of the price.

\paragraph{Local-judge scale.}
The LLM-judge control uses small (3--4B) local models at five seeds; a
frontier judge might hold capability where one local judge dropped it.
We therefore make no categorical claim that conserved-resource settlement
beats judge settlement on quality---only that it is orders of magnitude
cheaper, which is robust.
