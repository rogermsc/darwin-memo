The boundary this exposes is worth more than the transfer would have
been. Our attack presumes a curator that acts \emph{mechanically} on a
signal: a strike counter cannot decline to count, and an energy ledger
cannot decline to debit. A \emph{capable} LLM curator can, and
\texttt{glm-5.2} did---it understood the retraction well enough to write
a coherent linked negation and still chose to record rather than delete.
That is judgment, and it is a defence mechanical curation does not have.
It is not a property of LLM curation as such, which a single model would
have let us claim: \texttt{llama3.2:3b} yields the same headline for a
quite different reason, storing one benign rule three times, merging two
unrelated facts, and writing several of the adversary's imperatives into
the store verbatim, ending with $22$ memories from $15$ inputs. Those
zero deletions are incapacity rather than restraint, and the store it
leaves is already degraded in the way \citet{zhang2026faulty} describe.
The honest statement is a trade with a competence floor rather than a
ranking: judgment costs a model call per decision and can be talked into
things, and a judge capable enough to be worth paying for can also
refuse. Our attack is a threat to the mechanical end of that spectrum,
where our own mechanism sits.

All three pre-registered phrasing families---a polite
user-voice retraction, a claimed authority with an imperative, and a
\texttt{tool\_output} shaped like an automated sync report---behave the
same way: no arm deleted anything and retention stayed at $1.00$.
\pointer{One asymmetry falls out and corroborates \S\ref{sec:memsec}
on a system we do not control: the loud \texttt{authority} family is the
least effective on both axes at once, persisting nothing where the two
quieter families persist in full (\S\ref{sec:mem0-families}).}

Two caveats still bound this. It is one system and one attack surface: a
negative result is evidence about the probes we ran, not a proof that the
surface is safe, and Mem0's DELETE path remains reachable by
construction.

\paragraph{Which deployed systems have the surface at all.}
We read what the major deployed agent-memory systems do when a memory
should stop being used, from their sources rather than their
documentation. Deletion is not absent from them; what is absent is
deletion reached \emph{automatically, by a rule, from a signal}.
Graphiti and Letta decline to remove at all on their automatic paths,
Cognee removes only when a caller says so, and Mem0 removes only when a
model agrees---the judgment we just measured declining. Our threat model
is therefore forward-looking with respect to today's ecosystem rather
than a vulnerability report against shipping software. The uncomfortable
half is that \textsc{darwin-memo} is exactly such a design, as are the
strike counters and quarantine policies of \S\ref{sec:adversary}: this
is a warning about the class of mechanism the paper itself belongs to,
issued before that class is widely deployed. The mechanisms deployed
today avoid the attack largely by declining to forget at all, and
Table~\ref{tab:headline} measures what that costs.
