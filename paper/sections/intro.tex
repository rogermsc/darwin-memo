\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A frozen language model can be given new knowledge without retraining by
attaching an external memory: a store of past experience that is
retrieved into the context window at inference time
\citep{packer2023memgpt, park2023generative, shinn2023reflexion,
zhao2024expel}. \citet{quek2026memo} formalize the strong version of this
idea---memory \emph{as a model}, a dedicated artifact that holds knowledge
while the executive LLM stays fixed. Every such system, structured store
or learned memory model alike, faces the same question once it runs long
enough: \emph{what gets to stay?} Memory that only grows is eventually
dominated by stale, redundant, or actively wrong entries, and recent work
shows the failure is not hypothetical---memory banks that an LLM
continuously rewrites degrade over time, with useful entries becoming
faulty as the rewriting accumulates \citep{zhang2026faulty}.

The prevailing answer puts a \emph{model in the loop} to curate. An LLM
rates importance \citep{park2023generative}, rewrites and consolidates
trajectories \citep{zhang2026faulty}, or acts as a judge that decides
what to keep; alternatively a learned reward model scores entries. All of
these share two costs. First, they are expensive: every curation decision
is a model call. Second, and more fundamentally, they introduce a
\emph{proxy} between the agent and the world. A judge grades plausibility
and prose, not consequences, and a proxy that can be optimized against
can be gamed---the reward-hacking and semantic-drift failure mode that
self-training systems are known to fall into when no external criterion
anchors quality \citep{dodgson2026survival}.

This paper asks what happens when the curator is removed entirely. Our
starting observation is that a large and practically important class of
agent tasks comes with a \emph{conserved, physically measurable resource}
that already settles the question of value without anyone grading
anything. A storage-management agent frees or wastes real bytes. A coding
agent makes a test suite pass or fail. An issue-resolution agent resolves
a GitHub issue or does not. In each case the environment, not a model,
returns a scalar that is \emph{conserved} (you cannot manufacture freed
bytes or passing tests by being persuasive) and therefore cannot be
hacked by a more eloquent memory. Building on the environment-mediated
selection principle of \citet{dodgson2026survival}, we make that scalar
the only currency of memory survival.

\paragraph{\textsc{darwin-memo}.}
Each memory entry carries an \emph{energy} balance. Every cycle, every
entry pays a fixed upkeep, so existence is not free. An entry earns energy
back only when a task it decided (or supported) produces a positive
measured resource delta, with credit flowing along provenance and bounded
by a $\tanh$ so no single outcome makes an entry immortal and no single
disaster instantly kills one that has been right many times. When an
entry's energy crosses zero it is buried. The consequences fall out of the
dynamics rather than from any rule about quality: trivia nobody consults
starves on upkeep; a poisoned entry that advises a harmful action is
\emph{executed by the damage it causes} as the negative delta flows back
to it; near-duplicate survivors consolidate, pooling energy. There is no
reward model, no LLM judge, and no human curation anywhere, and because
settlement is a scalar the environment already returns, it is essentially
free.

\paragraph{What we claim, and what we do not.}
It would be easy, and wrong, to claim that conserved-resource selection
dominates all alternatives. It does not. On a deterministic environment
a one-line heuristic---``evict whatever just caused a negative
outcome''---matches the full energy ledger on outcomes; in a second
environment family that charges nothing for refusing to act, a strike
counter beats it below a noise threshold and a quarantine policy beats it
above; and a Thompson-sampling retrieval bandit reconstructed from
\citet{xu2026ael} matches it under one-sided noise. We report all of
these. Our contribution is the \emph{regime map}: a precise account,
backed by pre-registered experiments, of \emph{when} conserved-resource
selection is the right tool and where cheaper or different mechanisms win.
The properties that survive every comparison in our study are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It starves dead weight.} The energy ledger is the only
  mechanism we test that removes never-consulted entries; heuristics and
  the bandit hoard them indefinitely.
  \item \textbf{It removes poisoned entries completely}, acting and
  dormant alike. In the deterministic suites it kills actionable poison
  within one cycle, where a confidence bandit needs several confirmed
  failures and a recency policy never kills it at all. That speed does
  not survive a model in the loop: a one-line eviction counter revokes
  in one to three cycles against the ledger's eight to ten
  (\S\ref{sec:wef}). What the ledger retains there is completeness---it
  is the only arm that ends with no poisoned entry alive---and it pays
  for it in latency.
  \item \textbf{It tolerates lying measurements}, including adversarial
  ones. A continuous energy buffer with earn-back forgives transient
  false signals that linearly consume any strike counter, and it is the
  only mechanism we test that withstands an attacker aiming at the
  curator itself rather than at the memory---one corrupted measurement
  per cycle destroys every counter's benign capability and costs the
  ledger nothing.
  \item \textbf{It needs no labels and no judge}, and so settles
  $10^4$--$10^6\times$ more cheaply than an LLM-judge or LLM-generator
  arm doing the same per-cycle work.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A label-free, judge-free memory-curation mechanism that grounds
survival in a conserved environmental resource, with credit assignment
along provenance and a formal description (\S\ref{sec:method}).
(2) A reproducible, stdlib-only evaluation harness with pre-registered
hypotheses, seed-level bootstrap intervals, exact paired permutation
tests, and committed per-seed data validated against a manifest in CI
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
(3) Control arms reconstructed from the literature---a retrieval bandit
\citep{xu2026ael}, an LLM-judge settler, and a Generative-Agents-style
salience score \citep{park2023generative}---run on the same harness
rather than argued about.
(4) A threat model the memory-security literature has not defined---the
\emph{curation-targeted attack}, in which the adversary corrupts the
signal a curator decides on in order to starve the defender's benign
memory---together with the first measurement of how existing curation
mechanisms fare under it (\S\ref{sec:adversary}).
(5) The regime map itself (\S\ref{sec:regime}): an honest, boundary-aware
statement of where the mechanism wins, ties, and loses, including
published failure boundaries under measurement noise and attack.
