\begin{abstract}
External memory lets a frozen language-model agent accumulate knowledge
without retraining, but every such system must decide what to keep. The
dominant answer is a \emph{model in the loop}: an LLM rates, rewrites, or
judges memories, or a learned reward model scores them. We show that on
tasks with a \emph{conserved, measurable resource}---disk bytes, passing
tests, resolved issues---the curator can be removed entirely. We present
\textsc{darwin-memo}, in which each memory entry pays an energy upkeep
every cycle and earns energy back only when the environment measures a
positive outcome along its provenance. Useless entries starve; poisoned
entries that drive harmful actions are executed by the damage they cause;
near-duplicates consolidate. There is no reward model, no LLM judge, and
no human curation anywhere, and settlement is therefore essentially free.
Our contribution is not that this beats every alternative---it does not,
and we report where it loses. It is a \emph{regime map}: across two
environment families, ablations, measurement-noise sweeps, and control
arms reconstructed from the literature (a Thompson-sampling retrieval
bandit, an LLM-judge settler, and a hand-designed salience score), we
characterize precisely when conserved-resource selection dominates and
where a one-line heuristic or a confidence bandit matches or beats it.
The differentiators that survive every comparison are sharp: it starves
dead weight, kills actionable poison within one cycle, tolerates lying
measurements that defeat strike counters, requires no labels, and settles
$10^4$--$10^6$ times more cheaply than an LLM judge or generator. We
also define a threat model the memory-security literature has not---the
\emph{curation-targeted attack}, where the adversary corrupts the signal
the curator decides on in order to starve the defender's benign
memory---and show that one corrupted measurement per cycle destroys the
benign capability of every strike counter and quarantine policy we test,
on all 30 seeds, at no cost to the ledger, whose own boundary we locate
and publish. Every
number is produced by a stdlib-only harness with pre-registered
hypotheses, seed-level bootstrap intervals, paired permutation tests, and
committed per-seed data validated against its manifest in CI.
