\begin{abstract}
Agent memory that only grows is eventually dominated by stale and
poisoned entries, so every practical system curates, and every curator
decides on some signal---a classifier's verdict, a judge's rating, a
measured outcome. The memory-security literature defends the store at
\emph{write} and at \emph{retrieval}, and both read content. Recent work
names a third possibility without measuring it: an adversary who attacks
the deciding signal itself. We make that threat quantitative. In a
\emph{curation-targeted attack} the adversary injects no poison at all
and instead corrupts settlement so that the defence starves the memory
it protects---denial of memory---and we measure six curation mechanisms
against it at five attack budgets over 30 seeds.

The separation is sharp and so is its boundary. At one corrupted
measurement per cycle, every strike counter and quarantine policy we
test loses most or all benign capability---an evict-on-negative counter
loses all of it---while a bounded-credit energy ledger loses none and
retains $99.4\%$ of its unattacked outcome. A Thompson-sampling bandit
also keeps capability, but only by never removing anything: its
poison-kill rate falls to $0.07$ by four lies per cycle. Retention is
not defence. The ledger's own failure boundary lies between two and four
lies per cycle and we publish it rather than stopping at the win. An
adversary that buys \emph{persistence} instead of destruction takes its
poison-kill rate from $1.00$ to $0.00$ (paired $p = 0.0039$) while
benign capability never moves, so the guarantee disappears without
anything changing in the metric an operator watches.

We are equally precise about where the mechanism is unnecessary. Absent
an adversary a one-line heuristic ties it on outcomes and a confidence
bandit matches it under one-sided noise; across $2{,}115$ evaluated
SWE-Bench-CL tasks no memory arm beat carrying no memory at all, and
what it buys there is leanness---the same capability from half the
store. Capability therefore cannot separate under attack on those tasks
either, but the mechanism still does, in its proper currency: the
counter's deletion of \emph{benign} memory roughly triples where the
ledger's barely moves. The ledger is \textsc{darwin-memo}, in which each
entry pays energy upkeep and earns only from a conserved resource the
environment already measures: no reward model, no judge, no labels. We
give the regime map that says which curator to use when, and report
every one of those losses. Every number comes from a stdlib-only harness
with pre-registered hypotheses, exact paired permutation tests, and
committed per-seed data validated against its manifest in CI.
\end{abstract}
