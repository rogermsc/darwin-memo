\begin{abstract}
Agent memory that only grows is eventually dominated by stale and
poisoned entries, so every practical system curates, and every curator
decides on some signal. The memory-security literature defends the store
at \emph{write} and at \emph{retrieval}, and both read content. Recent
work names a third possibility without measuring it: an adversary who
attacks the deciding signal itself. In a \emph{curation-targeted attack}
the adversary injects no poison and instead corrupts settlement so that
the defence starves the memory it protects---denial of memory---and we
measure six curation mechanisms against it at five budgets over 30 seeds.

The ledger that survives it is \textsc{darwin-memo}, in which each entry
pays energy upkeep and earns only from a conserved resource the
environment already measures: no reward model, no judge, no labels. At
one corrupted measurement per cycle it keeps all benign capability and
$99.4\%$ of its unattacked outcome, where the strike counters and
quarantine policies keep at most about half and an evict-on-negative
counter keeps none. A Thompson-sampling bandit also keeps capability, but
only by never removing anything: its poison-kill rate falls to $0.07$ by
four lies per cycle. Retention is not defence. We publish the ledger's
own failure boundary, between two and four lies per cycle, rather than
stopping at the win, and an adversary buying \emph{persistence} instead
of destruction takes its poison-kill rate from $1.00$ to $0.00$ (paired
$p = 0.0039$) while benign capability never moves---the guarantee
disappears without anything changing in the metric an operator watches.

Sweeping the counter family's whole dial refuted our own pre-registered
prediction: a forgiveness counter at $k{=}5$ reaches the ledger's corner
at two lies per cycle. It gets there by barely evicting, and pays on an
axis the corner does not price---$16\%$ less measured outcome from a
store nearly four times larger, at every budget ($p = 0.0003$).

We are equally precise about where the mechanism is unnecessary. Absent
an adversary a one-line heuristic ties it and a confidence bandit matches
it under one-sided noise; across $2{,}115$ evaluated SWE-Bench-CL tasks
no memory arm beat carrying no memory at all, and what it buys there is
leanness. What separates under attack on real tasks is an ordering rather
than a magnitude: the counter loses more benign memory in $5$ of $6$
paired worlds, but the tripling we measure on one repository is absent on
a second, so we report an effect scoped to one repository and claim
significance for neither half. We give the regime map that says which
curator to use when---including where a tuned counter or a one-line
heuristic is the right answer---and report every one of those losses.
Every number comes from a stdlib-only harness with pre-registered
hypotheses, exact paired permutation tests, and committed per-seed data
validated against its manifest in CI, including the prediction this paper
refuted.
\end{abstract}
